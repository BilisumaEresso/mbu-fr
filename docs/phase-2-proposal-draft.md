# Meki Batu Union

## Phase 2 Digital Business Opportunities

**General proposal draft for company discussion**  
**Status:** Draft for review and prioritization  
**Prepared:** October 2026

---

## 1. Executive summary

Meki Batu Union's website currently introduces the organization, its member
services, products, facilities, retail presence, impact, and commercial
enquiry channels. It is a portfolio and information resource; it does not yet
provide a complete digital system for managing the union's operations or
delivering ongoing online services to its members and customers.

Phase 2 is an opportunity to select one or more business priorities and shape
digital services around them. Possibilities include internal inventory and
workflow control, a farmer and cooperative portal, a buyer sourcing service,
an open agricultural marketplace, input distribution, produce traceability,
retail and wholesale services, and management reporting.

These are alternatives for company consideration, not a predetermined scope.
The union may select one option, combine related options, or begin with a
focused first release and expand later. No budget, implementation schedule,
service commitment, or performance target is assumed in this draft. Those
should be agreed after the company confirms its priorities, current processes,
operational readiness, and available resources.

**Decision requested:** Agree which business problem Phase 2 should solve
first, who the first users should be, and which outcome the company will use
to judge success.

## 2. Purpose and intended audience

This draft is intended to help the union's leadership and relevant business
teams discuss the possible direction of Phase 2 before commissioning a
detailed scope, budget, or delivery plan. It provides a shared description of
potential services and the business decisions each would involve.

It is deliberately solution-neutral. It does not prescribe a particular
platform, system design, vendor, budget, or implementation schedule.

## 3. Current position and opportunity

The current site describes Meki Batu Union as a farmer-owned organization
connecting member cooperatives and farmers with agricultural inputs,
production support, aggregation, storage, markets, buyers, and retail
customers. It presents domestic and export-facing produce and seed activity,
farmer services, infrastructure, and organizational impact.

Today, the site mainly supports awareness and initial enquiries. Phase 2
could build on that foundation by enabling selected business interactions to
be handled, followed up, and measured more consistently.

Potential business outcomes include:

- More reliable visibility of inventory, requests, deliveries, and work in
  progress.
- Clearer coordination between union staff, branches, facilities, and
  cooperatives.
- Easier access to relevant information and services for members.
- A more structured commercial experience for buyers and institutional
  customers.
- Better confidence in product quality, origin, availability, and fulfilment.
- More consistent information for management decisions and reporting.

## 4. Phase 2 objectives

The company may use the following objectives to assess options:

1. Improve a defined business process, rather than digitizing a process that
   has not yet been agreed.
2. Make responsibilities and hand-offs clear to the people doing the work.
3. Provide useful and appropriate services to the selected user groups.
4. Improve the availability, quality, and timeliness of business information.
5. Establish measurable outcomes that can be reviewed after launch.
6. Select a scope that the union can operate, support, and keep current.
7. Preserve the option to expand when the first service demonstrates value.

## 5. Guiding principles for choosing a scope

- **Start with a business need.** Define the users, the current problem, and
  the desired result before selecting features.
- **Keep the first release focused.** Prioritize a coherent, usable service
  over a long list of partially delivered capabilities.
- **Design around real roles and responsibilities.** Access and approval
  responsibilities should reflect how the union is governed and operated.
- **Use dependable information.** Stock, crop, price, certification, outlet,
  and contact information need clear owners and update practices.
- **Build trust.** Member, buyer, seller, and product information should be
  handled with appropriate consent, verification, and accountability.
- **Plan for day-to-day operations.** Identify who will manage requests,
  resolve issues, answer enquiries, and maintain content after launch.
- **Measure business results.** Agree a small number of indicators before
  implementation and establish a baseline where possible.

## 6. Phase 2 opportunity options

The following options can be considered individually or combined where there
is a clear business reason. Each option is described at a business level; the
precise scope would require discovery with the relevant teams.

### Option 1 — Internal inventory and operations management

**Purpose:** Give staff a consistent way to see and manage goods and work
across the union's operations.

**Potential users:** Warehouse and store teams, packhouse and logistics
teams, procurement, finance, department managers, and leadership.

**Possible capabilities:**

- Record goods received, stored, transferred, distributed, sold, or written
  off.
- Monitor produce, seeds, agricultural inputs, packaging, and other agreed
  inventory categories.
- Coordinate requests, tasks, approvals, and hand-offs between departments
  and locations.
- Show pending work, stock exceptions, and selected operational summaries.
- Assign responsibilities and approval limits according to agreed staff
  roles.

**Potential business value:** Better stock visibility, fewer discrepancies,
clearer accountability, more consistent processes, and improved ability to
plan purchasing and distribution.

**Key decisions and dependencies:** Confirm which locations and inventory
types are in scope; agree units, stock ownership, counting practices,
adjustment approvals, and the rules for shared or transferred goods. The
company should identify process owners and establish reliable opening
balances before relying on digital stock figures.

### Option 2 — Farmer and cooperative services portal

**Purpose:** Provide members and primary cooperatives with a practical hub
for relevant union services and information.

**Potential users:** Farmers, cooperative representatives, union member
services staff, agronomy teams, and administrators.

**Possible capabilities:**

- Maintain farmer and cooperative profiles and membership status.
- Submit and follow requests for inputs, machinery, training, or other union
  services.
- Record crop plans, expected harvests, produce deliveries, and relevant
  follow-up.
- Share seasonal announcements, training resources, market information, and
  service updates.
- Give members access to appropriate delivery, input, payment, or account
  information.

**Potential business value:** Easier communication, more visible request
handling, improved production planning, and better continuity of member
service.

**Key decisions and dependencies:** Define who may register, who confirms
membership, what information is appropriate to show to each user, and how
members without regular internet access will be supported. Agree how records
are corrected and how enquiries are handled.

### Option 3 — Buyer and commercial sourcing portal

**Purpose:** Help wholesale, institutional, and export buyers understand the
union's offer and move commercial enquiries toward a clear next step.

**Potential users:** Importers, wholesalers, supermarkets, hotels,
restaurants, institutional kitchens, union sales staff, and export teams.

**Possible capabilities:**

- Present products, seasonal supply windows, grades, packaging, and
  relevant quality or certification information.
- Collect buyer requirements for products, volumes, destinations, packaging,
  and delivery timing.
- Organize follow-up for quotes, samples, specifications, and commercial
  discussions.
- Let authorized buyers or staff review the status of an enquiry or order.
- Maintain buyer profiles and a record of prior sourcing discussions.

**Potential business value:** A clearer buyer experience, better-quality
enquiries, more organized sales follow-up, and improved visibility into
commercial demand.

**Key decisions and dependencies:** Agree which product and supply
information can be published, who confirms availability and prices, what
response commitments the sales team can support, and what constitutes a
confirmed order or supply agreement.

### Option 4 — Open agricultural marketplace

**Purpose:** Explore a broader digital market where multiple agricultural
sellers and buyers can discover potential trading opportunities.

**Potential users:** Cooperatives, producers, buyers, agricultural suppliers,
service providers, and marketplace administrators.

**Possible capabilities:**

- Publish listings or requests for agricultural products and selected
  services.
- Search and filter opportunities by product, location, season, volume, or
  other agreed criteria.
- Support contact and negotiation between verified participants.
- Provide listing standards, participant verification, and mechanisms to
  report problems.
- Add transaction or fulfilment support if the company chooses to take on
  that responsibility.

**Potential business value:** Broader market access and a potential
sector-level service beyond the union's own products and members.

**Key decisions and dependencies:** Determine who can participate, whether
the service is limited to union members or open to the sector, how listings
are verified, how disputes and misleading offers are handled, and how the
service will be funded and managed. Clarify whether the union is only
connecting parties or taking responsibility for payment, delivery, quality,
or transaction outcomes.

**Scope note:** This is the broadest and most externally dependent option.
It should be considered as a distinct business initiative, not assumed to be
a simple extension of the company website.

### Option 5 — Agricultural inputs and member purchasing

**Purpose:** Improve how seasonal input demand is collected, reviewed, and
fulfilled for member cooperatives.

**Potential users:** Farmers, cooperative representatives, input
distribution teams, procurement, finance, and partner suppliers.

**Possible capabilities:**

- Publish approved inputs, seasonal information, and applicable terms.
- Collect and consolidate requests from cooperatives.
- Review eligibility, stock, allocation, fulfilment, and outstanding
  requests.
- Record deliveries or collections and any relevant balances.
- Support advance demand planning and collective purchasing discussions.

**Potential business value:** More visible member demand, better seasonal
planning, clearer allocation decisions, and improved tracking of fulfilment.

**Key decisions and dependencies:** Confirm the input categories, allocation
rules, price and credit approval processes, supplier responsibilities, and
the relationship between the service and existing financial records.

### Option 6 — Produce quality, traceability, and delivery visibility

**Purpose:** Strengthen operational oversight and buyer confidence by
following agreed produce information through key handling stages.

**Potential users:** Cooperatives, receiving and grading teams, quality
assurance, packhouse and logistics teams, sales, and buyers.

**Possible capabilities:**

- Record cooperative or farm origin and delivery details.
- Follow agreed inspection, grading, packing, storage, and dispatch events.
- Record quality checks and exceptions for staff follow-up.
- Provide buyers with appropriate product-origin or handling information.
- Summarize delivery status and quality trends for internal review.

**Potential business value:** Better quality oversight, more transparent
handling information, improved investigation of issues, and stronger support
for commercial relationships.

**Key decisions and dependencies:** Agree what information is collected at
each step, who is responsible for recording it, how quality exceptions are
handled, and what evidence is needed to support claims made to buyers.
Traceability information should not be described as a certification or
independent guarantee unless it has been verified accordingly.

### Option 7 — Retail and wholesale customer services

**Purpose:** Make it easier for retail customers and commercial customers to
find relevant outlets, product information, and sales contacts.

**Potential users:** Household customers, branch teams, wholesale buyers,
institutional customers, and retail management.

**Possible capabilities:**

- Maintain accurate outlet details, opening hours, contact information, and
  directions.
- Share product availability or daily-arrival information where branches can
  keep it current.
- Collect enquiries or reservations for products and bulk orders.
- Help route wholesale or institutional requests to the appropriate sales
  contact.
- Review customer interests and enquiries by outlet or customer type.

**Potential business value:** More useful customer information, better
handling of sales enquiries, and improved visibility into customer demand.

**Key decisions and dependencies:** Determine whether branch teams can
maintain timely stock information, whether reservations can be fulfilled,
and how household retail differs from wholesale and institutional supply.
Online ordering should only be proposed if fulfilment, pricing, payment, and
customer support processes are ready.

### Option 8 — Management performance and impact reporting

**Purpose:** Give leadership a clearer, more consistent view of selected
business and member-service results.

**Potential users:** Union leadership, department managers, finance,
operations, member representatives, and authorized reporting teams.

**Possible capabilities:**

- Summarize agreed measures such as enquiries, orders, inventory,
  deliveries, input fulfilment, member-service requests, or outlet activity.
- Review information by period, product, location, cooperative, or
  department where appropriate.
- Prepare repeatable internal and external reports.
- Compare results with company-approved targets and identify exceptions.

**Potential business value:** Better management decisions, clearer
accountability, and more consistent reporting to members, partners, or
funders.

**Key decisions and dependencies:** Agree definitions and accountable data
owners for each measure. Reporting should follow reliable operating records;
it cannot compensate for incomplete, outdated, or inconsistent information.

## 7. Possible combinations

These combinations illustrate ways to group related options. They are
discussion starters, not recommendations or approved packages.

| Package | Options | Business focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A. Improve internal control | 1 + 8 | Inventory, operational coordination, and management visibility. |
| B. Strengthen member services | 2 + 5 | Cooperative access to union information, inputs, and service requests. |
| C. Grow commercial sales | 3 + 6 | Buyer sourcing supported by stronger quality and delivery visibility. |
| D. Develop retail and institutional sales | 3 + 7 | Commercial enquiries and customer services across retail and bulk sales. |
| E. Explore a sector marketplace | 2 + 3 + 4 | Connections between members, buyers, and potentially wider market participants. |

Combinations should be selected only where users, processes, ownership, and
information needs fit together. A package can also be divided into stages,
provided each stage delivers a useful service on its own.

## 8. Suggested prioritization approach

The company can assess each option against the following questions:

1. **Business importance:** How significant is the problem or opportunity?
2. **User need:** Who needs the service, and how frequently?
3. **Expected value:** What business result could improve, and how would it
   be measured?
4. **Readiness:** Are the relevant processes, information, and
   responsibilities sufficiently clear?
5. **Operational ownership:** Which team will operate the service and
   respond to users?
6. **Adoption:** Can intended users access and use the service in their
   normal working conditions?
7. **Risk and responsibility:** What financial, commercial, privacy,
   regulatory, or reputational responsibilities would the union take on?
8. **Expandability:** Can the initial choice support useful future services
   without making the first release too broad?

The company may rate each factor as high, medium, or low, then discuss the
trade-offs. A simple score can help organize discussion but should not replace
leadership judgment or operational consultation.

## 9. Recommended next-phase planning process

### Stage 1 — Company direction

Leadership selects the primary business outcome, initial user group, and
preferred option or combination. It also identifies any non-negotiable
constraints or business commitments.

### Stage 2 — Business discovery

Relevant staff and users describe how the current process works, where the
main difficulties occur, what information is required, and what a successful
outcome would look like. This stage should include the teams expected to
operate the service, not only decision-makers.

### Stage 3 — Scope and readiness

The company confirms the first release's business boundaries, ownership,
approval responsibilities, operating procedures, information quality,
training needs, and user support approach. Any claims or source information
that will be shown to users or buyers are verified.

### Stage 4 — Proposal and approval

The agreed scope is translated into a detailed proposal covering
deliverables, exclusions, responsibilities, assumptions, costs, risks,
delivery stages, acceptance measures, and ongoing operating requirements.
The company reviews and approves this proposal before delivery begins.

### Stage 5 — Pilot and review

Where appropriate, the service is introduced to a defined group, location,
or business process first. The company reviews adoption, user feedback,
operational issues, and agreed performance measures before deciding whether
to expand.

## 10. Business readiness and risks to address

Before committing to a particular option, the company should consider:

- **Process clarity:** Are the real-world steps, exceptions, and approval
  points understood and agreed?
- **Information quality:** Are membership, stock, product, pricing,
  location, and delivery records accurate and assigned to an owner?
- **Roles and accountability:** Who may view, submit, approve, change, or
  resolve each type of business activity?
- **Adoption and access:** Do users have the language, devices, connectivity,
  training, and support needed to participate?
- **Commercial responsibility:** Who confirms prices, availability, delivery
  commitments, quality statements, and final order terms?
- **Privacy and trust:** What personal, financial, or commercially sensitive
  information is necessary, who may use it, and how will concerns be
  resolved?
- **Ongoing ownership:** Who maintains the service, answers enquiries,
  resolves issues, reviews access, and keeps public information current?
- **External participation:** If suppliers or non-member market participants
  are involved, what verification and participation rules apply?
- **Service continuity:** How will important work continue if the online
  service is temporarily unavailable or a user cannot access it?

These are business planning considerations, not a substitute for professional
legal, financial, operational, or regulatory advice where such advice is
needed.

## 11. Candidate measures of success

The company should choose only measures relevant to the selected scope and
establish current baselines where possible. Examples include:

| Business area | Example measures |
| --- | --- |
| Inventory and operations | Stock-record accuracy; time to complete receiving or transfer records; number of unresolved stock exceptions; order or task turnaround time. |
| Farmer and cooperative services | Active participating cooperatives; request fulfilment rate; response time; member satisfaction; accuracy of delivery or input records. |
| Buyer sourcing | Qualified enquiries; time to first response; quote conversion; repeat buyer activity; order fulfilment against agreed terms. |
| Marketplace | Verified participants; active listings; successful introductions; repeat activity; reported disputes and resolution time. |
| Inputs and purchasing | Requests reviewed; allocation and fulfilment rates; timeliness of delivery; unresolved requests; member satisfaction. |
| Quality and traceability | Deliveries with complete agreed records; quality exceptions identified and resolved; buyer information requests completed. |
| Retail and wholesale | Enquiries by customer type; branch information accuracy; bulk enquiries completed; repeat commercial activity. |
| Management reporting | Timeliness of agreed reports; completeness of source information; consistency of definitions; management use of reports. |

Targets should be set by the company after reviewing the baseline, intended
users, and operating capacity. This draft intentionally does not assign
numerical targets.

To move from this general draft to a specific Phase 2 proposal, the company

1. Which business problem or opportunity should Phase 2 address first?
2. Which option or combination should be explored?
3. Who are the first intended users?
4. Which locations, departments, product categories, or cooperatives should

## 13. Items to verify before formal approval

The current website is useful as an overview of the union's stated business
and services, but it should not be treated as the definitive source for
operational baselines. Before setting Phase 2 scope or publishing service
commitments, the company should confirm:

- Current counts of farmers, cooperatives, operating districts, outlets,
  facilities, and relevant assets.
- Product availability, supply seasons, volumes, specifications, packaging,
  pricing, and delivery commitments.
- The status and scope of certifications, licenses, traceability practices,
  and buyer or partner relationships.
- Which reports and downloadable documents are current and accurately
  described.
- The accuracy of operational, financial, impact, and historical claims.

For example, the retail page describes five Addis Ababa stores, while the
website's outlet listings contain eight entries. The company should confirm
the current outlet count and which locations are active before using either
figure as a planning baseline. News and reports should likewise be reviewed
for currency and accuracy.

## 14. Closing

Phase 2 can take several valid directions, from strengthening internal
inventory control to building member services, supporting commercial sales,
or exploring a marketplace for the wider agricultural sector. The best
choice depends on the union's current priorities, users, processes, readiness,
and capacity to operate the resulting service.

The recommended immediate action is to select a priority option or
combination and authorize a focused business-discovery stage. That work can
produce a company-approved, costed, and measurable Phase 2 proposal without
prematurely committing the union to a specific system or an overly broad
scope.
